Canopy interpretation
=====================

Unzip your assignment into a private folder. Open viewer.html in Chrome or
Firefox with JavaScript enabled. Work at a computer with a mouse or trackpad.
Enter the assigned code privately supplied by the coordinator. Use the Case menu
to move through the assignment. Work independently and use only the imagery
in the packet. NAIP imagery provided by USDA Farm Service Agency.

The columns show the earlier and later acquisition dates. The upper row is RGB.
The lower row is the same near-infrared/red/green composite at both dates.
Both dates use a nominal 0.6 m grid. Registration notes describe displacement
measured on features in the parent window, where available. The 2025 imagery
is averaged to that grid. No extra shift is applied. Tile dates have limited
flight-strip precision. Small position, color and shadow differences can be
image effects.

The dashed square is the 120 m scoring area. Use the full supplied context to
trace across it when a crown or loss component continues past the dashed line.
Follow the visible trace as far as the crop permits and flag continuation past
the crop. The square lacks the 120 m of surrounding context proposed for later
evaluation. Toggle the scoring boundary and traces for a clean image view.

1. On each date, trace overhead tree crowns visible within the scoring square
   and the visible continuation of anything that crosses it. Trace unknown
   areas on the corresponding date if shadows, leaf state, seam/no-data,
   displacement, or other obscuration prevent a reliable interpretation.
   Treat other readable areas as noncrown. Use multiple polygons if necessary.
   For an interior hole, trace pieces that leave the hole open.
2. On the early-date image, trace the part of each early crown that is clearly
   absent later. Keep separate loss fragments. Trace loss at any size you can
   interpret. An unknown area does not establish loss. The two dates establish
   a change in visible cover, with cause and exact removal date undetermined.
3. Select a trace layer, click vertices on its date's RGB or NIR view, and
   choose Finish polygon. The last vertex is automatically joined to the first.
   Use Undo vertex during drawing. To correct a saved trace, remove it and
   redraw. Inspect the traces and the unmarked views before finishing the case.
4. Mark a finished case Read complete, even if its loss mask is empty. Mark
   Unreadable if the case as a whole cannot be interpreted. Record active
   minutes for both. If you skip a case, leave its geometry empty and record
   active minutes if you spent time on it. Enter confidence for each completed
   interpretation. In Reasons, identify affected date and approximate location
   when explaining unknown patches or crop truncation, and explain unreadable
   cases. You may also note why a complete read was difficult or why you skipped.
5. Download return when finished. Send the JSON only to the coordinator through
   the private one-to-one route supplied with your assignment. For a break,
   download a draft to your private folder and use Resume from file when you
   return. Keep your final copy until the coordinator verifies receipt.

Adjudicator only
----------------

The adjudication assignment contains cases flagged by the comparison. Use
Original traces to inspect one reader at a time. Use the Trace menu to switch
which original layer is shown. Original overlays are dashed cyan and read-only. Hide them as needed and trace your own decision.
Record the rationale, disposition, and active adjudication minutes. Mark a
case Unresolved if the available imagery cannot settle the disagreement.
If it remains unreadable or is skipped, leave geometry and confidence empty.
Return your separate JSON privately to the coordinator.
