### Staffing and decision

For queue precision, a binomial one-sided 95% lower bound of 0.90 requires at least 114 confirmations in 120 or 169 in 180. At true precision 0.95, those designs have approximately 61% and 81% chances of passing. This calculation assumes independent, equally likely cases and a large queue. Use the actual finite frame and frozen queue budget for the study. Even 180 cases cannot establish useful yield if the eligible queue is too small or reviews take too long.

For recall sizing, start with 1,440 cells and assumed stratum proportions 0.10 / 0.15 / 0.75, loss-cell prevalence 0.40 / 0.18 / 0.02, and conditional match rates 0.96 / 0.85 / 0.05. The working approximation has at most one anchored event per cell. For assumed overall recall `r`, approximate the cell residual as `matched - r × true`, and sum `W_h² × (1 − n_h/N_h) × Var_h(residual)/n_h` over strata. Multiply the variance by a 1.5 sensitivity factor for extra variability, divide by squared mean event prevalence, and use 1.96 standard errors for the planning half-width. The three allocations yield approximately ±0.14, ±0.085 and ±0.069 at assumed recall 0.76. Halving prevalence in every stratum moves the 750-cell design to ±0.12 and the 885-cell design to ±0.10. Doubling nonloss prevalence alone drops assumed recall to 0.65. The pilot must measure prevalence including nonloss, multiple events per cell, clustering, event and queue sizes, unknown and repeat rates, time, and losses from the valid frame. Recompute allocations with observed inputs before adopting the precision and yield criteria.
